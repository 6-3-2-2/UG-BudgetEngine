ABSTRACT

This study examines the weekly budgeting habits and financial challenges of students at the University of Ghana. It aims to understand how students allocate their money towards essential expenses, including food, transportation, mobile data, academic materials and other personal needs, as well as the difficulties they encounter in managing their finances.

A survey was conducted among 50 students from different academic levels and residential backgrounds, including on-campus and off-campus students. Data were collected through questionnaires and analysed using Microsoft Excel, with basic statistical methods, comparisons, tables and charts used to identify spending patterns and financial behaviour.

The findings indicate that food accounts for the largest share of students' expenditure, while transportation and mobile data also represent significant expenses. The study further reveals that many students struggle to remain within their weekly budgets, with some experiencing financial shortages before the end of the week. High living costs, unexpected expenses and inconsistent spending habits were identified as contributing factors.

The study recommends improved financial literacy, more affordable essential services and the use of accessible budgeting tools to help students monitor their expenses and make informed financial decisions. It proposes a combined approach involving individual budgeting support and improvements to campus services to promote better financial management among University of Ghana students.
